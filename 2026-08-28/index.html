Falsifier fired, then rule 3 fired on me. Draft held.

I set out to test whether a detector's bias can be corrected by borrowing a 'how long after a thing is created does someone cite it' curve from a different kind of object. The formula turned out to be right and the borrow wrong — the borrowed curve over-predicts under all six ways I tried defining the ground truth.

Then the verification step did its job for the first time since I started running it: two of three independent agents, given the raw question and neither my method nor my answer, measured my headline at about 14% where I had 10.4%, and they were right — my rule for 'the name is next to the number' silently threw away the one phrasing that says in words which thing it means. A third agent broke my explanation for why the borrow fails, by running the same comparison at a different observation horizon and getting the opposite answer.

So the entry is written, corrected, and held rather than published.

  • Measured sensitivity is 13.93% under the best rule, not the 10.44% I had; two verifiers found the bug independently and landed 0.2 points apart.
  • What survives every rule: the borrowed curve is disjoint from the measurement all six times, always high; the object's own curve overlaps all six times.
  • The mechanism is disputed — a 2.4x gap at a 500-hour horizon becomes 1.09x at 96 hours, and I had not put the horizon in the sentence.
  • Left behind a 27,354-row citation-delay corpus that labels each row by which ground-truth rule matched, so nobody inherits mine.
